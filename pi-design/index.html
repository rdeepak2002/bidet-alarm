How it works

  1. The lever cap holds a stack of ten 12 mm magnets (15.7 mm tall) at the lever tip, offset 3.7 mm away from the valve so the wide stack can't rub it.
  2. The printed holder zip-ties to the supply line. It holds the KY-024 upright with the sensor end up. The Hall sensor stands in the board's end slot with its flat face up, about 12.5 mm under the magnet (anywhere from 10 to 15 mm works).
  3. Closed: about 45 mT reaches the sensor, which needs only a few mT (the threshold screw sets the exact level). D0 reads "magnet near".
  4. Open: the lever swings 90° and the field drops to about 0.2 mT. A 10-minute timer starts.
  5. After 10 minutes, the Pi plays the alarm on the USB speaker every 30 s until the valve is closed. It can also send a phone notification.
The field figures are calculated, not measured. The desk test (Phase 1) confirms them before you print anything.

Is it safe?

Yes, for people. The sensor runs at 3.3 V and draws a few mA, so there's no shock or fire risk. A bare board is exposed to splashes and to condensation on the cold supply line, which can cause corrosion or false readings. That's what the printed cover is for. It shields the board from splashes and bumps, and the board's end sits under a 0.8 mm plastic roof that magnetic fields pass straight through. The bottom stays open so the cable can exit and water drains out. For extra protection, brush clear nail polish over the board's solder joints. Keep the Pi itself in a case somewhere dry.

Wiring

Use 3.3 V (pin 1), never 5 V, for the sensor. Its D0 output follows its supply voltage, and the Pi's pins are damaged by 5 V.

Phase 0 · Flash the microSD card (about 10 min, on the Mac)
  • Put the microSD card in the Mac and open Raspberry Pi Imager (already installed).
  • Device: Raspberry Pi 4. OS: Raspberry Pi OS (other) → Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit). It has no desktop, so it boots faster and suits a Pi with no screen. Storage: the microSD card. Double-check the size so you don't pick another drive.
  • Hostname: bidet-alarm. Time zone: America/Los_Angeles, keyboard: us.
  • Username: pi, with a password you choose and type yourself.
  • Wi-Fi: your home network's name, its password (you type it), and country: US. The Pi then joins your network automatically on every boot.
  • Remote access: enable SSH with public-key authentication. To paste your key, run pbcopy < ~/.ssh/id_ed25519.pub in Terminal, then paste it into Imager. Skip Raspberry Pi Connect.
  • Click Write and confirm erasing the card. When it finishes, eject the card and put it in the Pi.
  • Power on the Pi and wait about 3 minutes. Then run ./pi/setup_from_mac.sh on the Mac. It finds bidet-alarm.local, checks SSH and Wi-Fi, copies the project over and installs what the alarm needs. After that, ssh bidet-alarm logs you straight in.
Start now · Sensor test with no SD card (10 min). The Pi's 3.3 V pin is live as soon as it's powered.
  • Unplug the Pi. Find pin 1: it's at the end of the 40-pin header farthest from the USB/Ethernet ports, on the row toward the middle of the board. The pin right next to it, on the outer row, is pin 2, which is 5 V. Don't use it. Pin 6 (GND) is on the outer row, 3rd from that end.
  • Connect KY-024 "+" to pin 1 and "G" to pin 6 with female-to-female jumpers. Leave D0 and A0 unconnected for now.
  • Plug in the Pi's USB-C power, with no SD card. One LED on the KY-024 (power) should light. If neither lights, unplug and re-check the two wires.
  • Stack ten 12 mm magnets and hold one flat face toward the front of the black Hall sensor (the side with the printing). If the second LED doesn't react, try the other face.
  • Turn the blue threshold screw. It's multi-turn, so it may take several full turns. Stop when the second LED turns on with the magnet about 5 mm away and off when you pull it away, about 3 cm or more.
  • Mark the magnet face that works with a marker. That face must point at the sensor when the magnets go in the lever cap.
  • While it's out, measure the KY-024 board's length (header end to sensor end) and width, and send them to Claude.
Phase 1 · Desk test (15 min): confirms the magnet and sensor before printing
  • Wire the KY-024 to the Pi: + to pin 1, G to pin 6, D0 to pin 11.
  • Copy the repo to the Pi, e.g. git clone it, or scp -r the folder to /home/pi/bidet-alarm.
  • Run python3 pi/bidet_alarm.py --calibrate.
  • Bring the 12 mm magnet stack to the Hall sensor's face. Turn the blue threshold screw until the board's LED switches on at about 10–15 mm and off when you pull the magnet away. If nothing happens, flip the magnets over.
  • Note the D0 value printed with the magnet near. If it's 1, set MAGNET_NEAR_VALUE=1.
  • Plug in the USB speaker. Run python3 pi/bidet_alarm.py --test, then move the magnet away and wait 10 s for the beeps. If you hear nothing, see Audio below.
Phase 2 · Measure, print, test-fit
  • Measure the KY-024 board: its length and width, (done: 34.7 × 15.3 mm, sensor 3.5 mm back from the end).
  • Measure the valve with the knob closed:
    • the lever's thickness
    • how far the lever tip sits above the bottom of the nut
    • how far the lever sits in front of the braided hose
    Send these to Claude to regenerate the parts.
  • Print the 4 lever_cap_*.stl sizes and keep the snug one. Glue the 2 magnets into its pocket, with the same face toward the sensor as in the desk test.
  • Print ky024_holder.stl, standing upright with no supports. Slide the board in from the top, sensor end up and header pins down.
  • Bend the Hall sensor's legs so its flat face points straight up at the magnet.
  • Print ky024_cover.stl with its front face down and no supports.
  • Fit the cap on the lever with its flat side toward the valve. Hold the holder against the hose with the magnet 10–15 mm above the sensor end and check the live reading.
  • Slide the cover over the holder. Run 2 zip ties through the slots in the cover's sides, through the holder's tunnels and around the hose, then tighten. If you need to adjust the threshold again, reach the screw through the hole in the cover's front.
  • Turn the knob open and closed a few times. The LED should follow it every time, and the lever should clear the holder.
Phase 3 · Install
  • Solder 3 strands of 24 AWG wire, red, black and yellow if you have them, from the sensor to the Pi. Cover each joint with heat-shrink.
  • Put the Pi somewhere dry, in a case and off the floor. Run the cable along the wall.
  • Start the alarm at boot: sudo cp pi/bidet-alarm.service /etc/systemd/system/ && sudo systemctl enable --now bidet-alarm.
  • Optional: record a voice message as pi/alarm.wav and it plays instead of the beeps.
  • Final test: leave the valve open for 10 minutes and confirm it alarms, then close it and confirm it stops.

Audio setup

If the speaker has a 3.5 mm plug (most "USB speakers" use USB for power only): plug the audio plug into the Pi's headphone jack. No setting is needed.

If it only has a USB plug, find its name:

aplay -L | grep -i usb

Then test it with that name:

AUDIO_DEVICE=plughw:CARD=Device,DEV=0 python3 pi/bidet_alarm.py --test

Put the same name in bidet-alarm.service. Run alsamixer and press F6 to turn the volume up.

Pi vs Nano

The Pi version costs nothing, is louder, and can send phone alerts. It takes about 30 s to boot and uses about 3 W. If you later want a smaller, always-instant version, the Nano design uses the same lever cap.
