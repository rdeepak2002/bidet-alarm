Mounting steps

  1. Print all four lever_cap_*.stl sizes. Push each onto the lever and keep the one that slides on snugly.
  2. Press the 6×3 mm magnet into the pocket in the cap's closed end, with a drop of superglue.
  3. Slide the cap onto the lever with its flat side facing the valve body (bonding it with a drop of superglue is optional). The magnet now sits at the lever tip.
  4. Push the reed sensor into the cradle's pocket, with the wires coming out the slot at the bottom.
  5. With the knob closed, hold the cradle against the supply line under the lever tip. Its V-groove goes against the hose.
  6. Slide it up until there's a 2–3 mm gap to the magnet. The recess at the top-back lets it ride up in front of the nut. Check that the Nano's LED is off when closed and on when open. Then loop 2 zip ties through the channels and tighten.
  7. Open the knob and check that the lever swings clear of the cradle.
  8. Run the sensor cable (yellow = D2, black = GND) down along the supply line, zip-tie it once more, then route it along the wall to the alarm box.

Box steps

  1. Solder wires straight to the Nano, without header pins, so it sits flat. Use these connections:
    FromTo Nano
    Reed sensor wire 1D2
    Reed sensor wire 2GND
    Buzzer +D8
    Buzzer −GND
  2. Slide the Nano onto the rails, USB end first, so its port lines up with the hole in the end wall.
  3. Press the buzzer into the ring under the lid, facing the grille holes.
  4. Drop the sensor cable into the slot in the other end wall, then press the lid on. It's a friction fit.
  5. Stick the box to the wall or the back of the tank with a Command strip or foam tape. Mount it up off the floor, away from splashes.

3 · Wiring

Wiring notes

  1. No resistors needed. The code turns on the Nano's internal pull-up on D2, so the reed switch goes straight between D2 and GND.
  2. The reed switch has no polarity, so its two wires can go either way round.
  3. The buzzer does have polarity. The + leg (the longer one, or marked "+") goes to D8.
  4. The Nano has two GND pins, one on each row. Use one for the sensor and the other for the buzzer.
  5. Extend the sensor's leads with 1–2 m of thin two-core wire to reach the box. Solder the joints and cover them with heat-shrink, since this is a damp room.
  6. Power comes from the USB cable and a phone charger. Nothing else connects to the Nano.
